# Monthly physical reward: design brief

This brief is for the designer of the Lorewalk coins. It says what to design, what the factory can make, and what to deliver.

## The project

Lorewalk is a walking game for Singapore. A player walks to real landmarks, checks in, and collects cats that hatch from eggs. Each month, a Premium player gets a challenge, such as "visit 5 landmarks in January".

A player who finishes the challenge earns a real metal coin. The player collects it in person at a monthly community walk in Singapore. The coin is the reason to subscribe, so it must feel worth the walk.

- There are 12 coins, one for each calendar month. The same 12 designs come back each year.
- A player who collects all 12 coins earns a ribbon medal. The medal is a later job and is not part of this brief.

## What to design

Design 13 faces: 12 fronts and 1 back.

- **Front**: different for each month. It shows the theme of that month.
- **Back**: the same on all 12 coins. It makes the coins one set.

Start with the back and one front. The other 11 fronts follow after the approval of those two.

## The coin

The supplier is Gift Market in Singapore. These values come from its product page for custom challenge coins.

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Die-cast metal alloy |
| Sides | A design on both sides |
| Diameter | 1.5, 1.75, or 2.0 inch (38, 44, or 51 mm). Not decided yet |
| Thickness | 3 mm |
| Finish | Antique gold |
| Color fill | None |

The size is not final. Make each design readable at 1.5 inch, the smallest size.

The coin has no color. The design is metal only: raised areas, recessed areas, and texture. The antique finish makes the recessed areas dark and the raised areas bright, so the relief does the work of contrast.

## The front

Each front shows one Singapore dish, tied to a festival or a season of that month. The app already shows a drawn medal for each month with the same themes. Open the app, go to the profile screen, and open "Monthly medals" to see them.

| Month | Dish | Festival or season | Phrase in the app |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | Bak kwa | Chinese New Year season | Huat Ah! |
| February | Yu sheng | Chinese New Year | Lo Hei! |
| March | Ketupat and rendang | Hari Raya Aidilfitri | Selamat! |
| April | Kueh lapis | Good Friday | Sedap Lah! |
| May | Ondeh-ondeh | Vesak Day | Namo! |
| June | Biryani | Hari Raya Haji | Makan Time! |
| July | Chilli crab | Singapore Food Festival | Power Lah! |
| August | Hainanese chicken rice | National Day | Majulah! |
| September | Mooncake | Mid-Autumn Festival | Steady! |
| October | Prata | Deepavali | Vandakam! |
| November | Laksa | Year-end walk | Tok Kong! |
| December | Pineapple tarts | Christmas | Ho Seh! |

Each front must have:

- The dish, as the main relief.
- The name of the month.

Each front can have:

- The phrase from the table, if it stays readable at 1.5 inch.

Do not put these on the front:

- The year. The designs come back each year, and a coin with a year cannot go to a player in the next year.
- The text of the challenge.
- The name of the festival. Some festivals move to a different month in some years, so the dish must work without it.

Several themes come from a religious festival. Show the dish, and keep sacred symbols and sacred text off the coin.

## The back

The back never changes, so it holds only things that are true for each month.

- The Lorewalk name or mark.
- A flat, blank area for a serial number, such as "No. 042". The supplier engraves the number on each coin by laser after the cast. The area needs room for 3 digits.
- No month and no year.

The creatures in the game are Singapore cats. A cat on the back is welcome but not necessary.

## The look

The app looks like a field journal: paper pages, ink outlines, red stamps, and postmarks. The coin must look like it comes from the same world. Think of a heritage token or a postmark, not a sports medal.

The 12 fronts must read as one family. Use the same border, the same lettering, and the same position for the month on each front.

The coin has no color, but a mockup or a presentation sheet can use the colors of the app.

| Name | Hex | Use in the app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#F5EEDC` | The page |
| Ink | `#33281E` | Text and outlines |
| Stamp red | `#A8361F` | Stamps and reward moments |
| Forest | `#2F6B4F` | Progress and success |
| Ochre | `#C98A1E` | Coins, medals, and Premium |

## What the factory can make

A die-cast coin cannot show everything that a drawing can.

- Use flat shapes at a small number of heights. Gradients and photo detail do not cast.
- Keep each line thick. A thin line fills in or breaks. The supplier gives the minimum line width on request.
- Keep text short and large. Small text becomes unreadable in metal.
- Keep text at least 1.5 mm from the edge.
- Use only fonts with a license for commercial use.

## What to deliver

1. Two or three sketch directions for the back and the first front.
2. One vector file for each approved face, as AI, EPS, or PDF. The supplier does not accept JPEG or PNG.
3. In each file: black and white artwork, text converted to outlines, and a note that marks which areas are raised, recessed, or textured.
4. One mockup of the front and the back at real size.

## Open points

- The deadline and the fee.
- The file of the Lorewalk mark for the back.
